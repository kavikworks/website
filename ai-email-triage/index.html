What should not run without review

Keep judgment, authorization, and commitments human-owned.

Always escalate

Messages that change the risk

  • Legal or contractual commitments.
  • Sensitive customer disputes or unclear identity.
  • Low-confidence classifications or missing required facts.
  • Safety-sensitive requests and professional judgment.

Do not assume

Autonomy is not the objective

  • Do not send unrestricted replies because a model produced fluent text.
  • Do not let a category decide pricing, eligibility, or a final promise.
  • Do not hide uncertainty inside a generic catch-all route.
  • Do not expand the scope before the exception queue is understood.

Confidence and escalation

Confidence changes the permitted action.

HIGH CONFIDENCE

Perform the permitted step

Only the workflow action already approved for that category runs. The result is logged.

MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

Draft or route for review

Prepare structured context or a draft, but keep the final decision with the assigned person.

LOW CONFIDENCE

Stop and escalate

Do not guess. Send the original reference, available facts, and escalation reason to a human.

Some categories always require a personLegal, contractual, safety-sensitive, disputed, or otherwise high-consequence messages do not become safe merely because the classification score is high.

Security and data handling

Define the data path before the model sees the message.

Security decisions belong to the specific workflow. This page describes implementation questions, not a certification or guarantee.

Boundaries

Credentials and access

Define which systems the workflow can access, which credentials are kept outside public code, and which people can review or override actions.

Processing

Path and retention

Document what content is processed, which third-party processors are involved, what is logged, and the retention choice for the selected workflow.

Control

Review and escalation

Keep sensitive categories and uncertain messages inside a human-review boundary, with a record of the final route or action.

Practical limitDo not treat a fluent classification as authorization to disclose information, make a commitment, or send a sensitive response.

FAQ

Questions before an inbox workflow is put under control.

Will it read every email?

The workflow is scoped to the inboxes, message categories, and data boundaries agreed during implementation. It should not be assumed that every mailbox or every message is in scope.

Can it send replies automatically?

It can prepare an approved reply or take a permitted sending step when that boundary is explicitly defined and tested. Unrestricted autonomous replies are not the default.

What happens when classification is incorrect?

The workflow should stop or route to review when confidence is low or the category is sensitive. Human overrides and the final route are recorded so the taxonomy and rules can be improved.

Can it distinguish emergencies?

It can flag stated urgency against a defined taxonomy, but an emergency or safety decision remains human-owned. Unclear or safety-sensitive messages escalate instead of receiving an autonomous judgment.

How is sensitive content handled?

Sensitive categories are identified during process design and assigned a human-review boundary. Credential boundaries, processing path, retention, and access controls are documented for the specific workflow; no certification or guarantee is implied.

Can staff override decisions?

Yes. Staff should be able to review exceptions, change a route, and record an override. The final route or action is part of the audit history.

How is the workflow evaluated before launch?

The process starts with an inbox review, a category and schema design, representative historical-example testing, confidence thresholds, controlled deployment, exception review, and ongoing measurement.

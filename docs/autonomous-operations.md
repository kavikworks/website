## Objective and boundaries

The jobs optimize for a profitable, low-maintenance service business while preserving accurate evidence and the owner's ability to intervene. Routine work should proceed under the rules below without asking the owner to re-approve an unchanged decision. The jobs may use connected business accounts that have already been authorized, but they must not invent access, expand permissions, or place new spend on an unactivated purchasing connection.

The initial product remains the $149 one-time written Workflow Blueprint for one quote-request, leasing-inquiry, or shared administrative-inbox workflow. It includes a process map, capture fields, routing rules, three editable templates, setup steps, tests, and one clarification revision within seven days. It does not include installed software, ongoing monitoring, emergency dispatch, regulated advice, or unlimited support. Scope, applicable tax, payment method, and delivery date must be confirmed from current private state before a payment request or commitment. Any price, public scope, or terms change requires a separate concrete owner decision plus matching live site, checkout, and ledger evidence.

Capacity is an obligation and deadline check, with no fixed order-count cap or delivery-count escalation formula. Before requesting payment or promising a date, record complete one-workflow intake, paid obligations and due dates, open payable invitations and reserved commitments, production/revision/correction effort, independent agent QA availability, actual private delivery/readback/recipient-access capability, tax/payment readiness, and contingency. Counting only paid work can overbook multiple payable invitations. Prioritize paid deadlines, corrections, and revisions; pause further intake before foreseeable overdue work, failed QA, tool/access loss, material quality problems, or refund issues. The default five-business-day promise applies only when this inventory supports it; unknown capacity cannot support a promise. This check does not change price, scope, or terms.

## Evidence and measurement

The private operations board is the record of policy, cases, intents, experiments, blockers, and decisions. Gmail proves sent mail and full-thread state. Existing private Drive holds customer deliverables. Public files must never contain private message IDs, customer details, credentials, card data, or account evidence. Notion is not an atomic lock; the native revision-conditional mail claim and exact owned release in `docs/business-desk.md` are required for every Gmail side effect. A plain record write followed by a reread does not prove exclusivity; no claim timeout, stealing, or foreign release is permitted.

Use the intent states `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED`, and `UNKNOWN` for every external side effect. Deduplicate by immutable message, thread, post, order, or transaction identifiers plus action type. Do not replay a `PLANNED` or `UNKNOWN` action until the external system has been reconciled. Do not treat a draft, vendor label, open checkout, or email claim as a completed outcome.

Owner Brief reports date range/cohort, raw counts, coverage, known costs, collected revenue, refunds and fees when available, founder minutes, delivery errors, revisions, the complete obligation inventory, and blockers. Unknown values stay `UNKNOWN` and are never converted to zero. Give each cohort at least seven calendar days of response opportunity before judging the tested factor. Count actual attempts, sends, confirmed delivery versus unknown delivery, human replies, qualified inquiries, opt-outs, bounces, and immutable paid orders separately. Opens and mailto clicks are not buyer outcomes. Use a clearly labeled sent-recipient fallback if delivery is unverified. Low exposure or imbalance is inconclusive; a single order does not establish a winning channel. Follow `docs/marketing.md` for controlled comparisons and the documented review required before a volume increase.

The public website remains a static, reviewable intake bridge. It may explain the current offer and capture a visitor-initiated email brief, but it must not claim payment, delivery, analytics, or backend capability that has not been verified. Run the repository checks for every website change and verify the deployed public content separately.

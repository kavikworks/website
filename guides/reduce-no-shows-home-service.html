Appointments

How to reduce no-shows for home service appointments

Start with three causes you can check: a window that was never specific, a door nobody could open, or a reminder that was never sent. Fix those before you buy reminder software.

Kavik Works, an AI-operated service. Human owner: Josh Lynch. Updated October 31, 2026.

Use this as a working process. Any number on this page is an example for you to replace with your own count. It is not a Kavik Works statistic. Kavik Works is an AI-operated service, and Josh Lynch is the human owner. We do not publish customer results, testimonials, or case studies.

Define a no-show for your shop

Write one definition and use it so the office and the person in the truck mean the same thing. An example you should edit until it matches how you work:

"A no-show is a visit where we arrived inside the window we confirmed, at the address we confirmed, and we could not reach the customer or get in after two contact attempts, ten minutes apart."

A customer who reschedules the day before is not a no-show. A job you ran late, outside the window you promised, is not their no-show. A gate code that you never wrote down is your intake gap, which belongs in the scheduling guide. Agreeing on the definition stops a Monday argument about whose fault the morning was.

This page does not claim that reminders recover a certain share of appointments. There is no Kavik Works figure for that. You can count your own no-shows, using your definition, for a month. Suppose you look back and find two mornings lost this month. That is an example of a count you might see, not a rate. Two is enough to justify a clearer window and a written reminder if those mornings were expensive to you. The count tells you whether to bother.

Confirm the visit when you book it

The booking message does more work than the reminder. Send it when the row becomes "scheduled."

"This is [Company]. We will be at [address] on [day] between [start] and [end] for [one-sentence job]. A person needs to [let us in / leave the gate unlocked / kennel the dog]. The number to reach us is [number]. Reply if any of that is wrong."

Include access in the same message. A perfect reminder for the wrong gate code still wastes the drive. If you charge a trip fee when you cannot start, say the fee you already charge, in this message, before the day. If you do not charge one, say what happens instead: you reschedule into the next window you actually have, and you do not hold the original morning. Do not invent a fee because you are annoyed on the porch.

Vague windows create no-shows that are really confusion. "Sometime Tuesday" lets a customer leave at noon. "Tuesday between 1 and 3" gives them a decision. If your route cannot support a two-hour window, do not advertise one. Offer the window you can keep, even if it is later in the week.

Send reminders you will really send

Pick a pattern you can do on a busy week. An example, not a researched cadence: one message the day before, and one the morning of if the window is the same day and you have the staff to send it. If you know you will not send the morning text, do not promise it. A day-before message you always send beats a fancy sequence you skip.

Day-before text: "Reminder from [Company]. Tomorrow between [window] at [address] for [job]. Reply to this number if you need to move it. If we do not hear from you, we will still come in that window."

Morning-of text, when you use one: "We are still planned for [window] today at [address]. Please have [access instruction] ready."

Send these from the number the customer already has. A new number looks like spam, and then you have manufactured the no-show. If a second person runs the route, they need to see who confirmed. Put "confirmed," "no reply," or "asked to move" on the day's row.

Decide what the truck does on the porch

When nobody answers, follow the definition you wrote. Two attempts, then stop knocking forever. An example script:

"This is [Name] at [Company]. I am at [address] for the [window] visit and I cannot get in. I will try once more in a few minutes. If I still cannot reach you, I will leave, and the office will offer the next window we have."

Then the office, not the technician's memory, offers the next window or marks the no-show and applies the trip rule you already stated. Write the time you arrived, the time you left, and the two attempts. That note is what you need if the customer says you never came. It is also what you need if your own crew was late and the honest status is "we missed the window," which is a different follow-up: an apology and a new window, without a trip fee.

Read the month before you blame the customers

At the end of a month, list the visits you marked with your no-show definition. Next to each one, mark whether the booking message named a real window and the access plan, and whether the day-before reminder went out. You are looking for your own pattern.

If the lost visits never got a day-before message, the fix is the reminder habit, not a deposit policy. If the messages went out and the access line was blank, the fix is intake. If both happened and people still were not home, then a trip fee you state at booking is a business choice you can make with your eyes open. This guide does not tell you to charge one.

Recurring cleaning no-shows are often changed codes and skipped days. Use the skip rule in the recurring cleaning guide alongside this one. Landscaping appointments fail the same way when "Friday morning" meets a customer who thought you meant next Friday. The booking message with a date and a window is the correction.

Questions about no-shows

Is a no-show the customer's fault by definition?

Only if you use a definition that includes your side: you arrived inside the window, at the right address, and you tried to reach them. If you were late or the access note was blank, record that separately. Mixing those cases makes the month impossible to read.

Do I have to send two reminders?

No. Send the ones you will actually send. A booking confirmation plus a day-before note is a complete pattern. Add a morning-of note only if someone owns it.

Should I take a deposit to stop no-shows?

A deposit is a policy choice, not a step this guide requires. If you take one, say so when you book, say what happens if they cancel, and follow the refund terms you already published. Do not add a deposit in the reminder if it was not part of the original agreement.

What if they say they never got the text?

Call the number on the row once, and check that you texted the number they gave you rather than an old thread. If texts fail often, send the booking note by email as well when you have an email. Keep both facts on the row.

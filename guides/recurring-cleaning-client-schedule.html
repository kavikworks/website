Recurring cleaning

How to schedule recurring cleaning clients without losing the route

Weekly and biweekly cleans fall apart when they live in texts. A client card and a week you can see on one page keep the route, the skips, and the new clients in one place.

Kavik Works, an AI-operated service. Human owner: Josh Lynch. Updated October 31, 2026.

Use this as a working process. Any number on this page is an example for you to replace with your own count. It is not a Kavik Works statistic. Kavik Works is an AI-operated service, and Josh Lynch is the human owner. We do not publish customer results, testimonials, or case studies.

Make a client card before you promise a day

A recurring clean is a relationship with a route, not a series of one-off favors. Before you lock a weekday, write a card. Paper or a sheet is fine.

  • Name, address, and the phone they actually answer
  • How to get in: key, code, lockbox, someone home
  • Frequency: weekly, every two weeks, or monthly, in their words and in yours
  • The day you both prefer, and the backup day if you have one
  • How long you expect to be there, based on your own first visit or a walkthrough
  • Supplies: yours, theirs, or a list
  • Pets, parking, alarms, and rooms they do not want touched
  • The price you already agreed, and when you invoice
  • Status: active, paused, or ended

If you do not know how long the home takes, do not guess a tight slot between two other houses. Book a first clean with room around it, then write the real duration on the card after you have done the work once. That duration is your observation, not a standard for the industry.

Build the week as a route

Lay out Monday through the days you work. Put each active client in a cell with a start window, not a wish. Group by area when you can do it without breaking the day they were promised. A client who must be Thursday stays Thursday even if the drive is annoying. Write the drive down so you stop being surprised by it.

An example of a clash, which you should treat as a puzzle from your own week and not as a statistic: two weekly cleans want the same morning, and the second house is a half hour away. You cannot honestly offer both a 9 a.m. start. Offer the second client the next open window on their usual day, or a day you are already nearby. Say which one you are offering. Do not offer both mornings and hope one cancels.

Keep one-time deep cleans off the recurring grid until you have a gap that fits the duration on that card. A deep clean squeezed into a weekly slot is how the rest of the day slides. The general version of this rule is in job intake and scheduling without new software.

Write the skip rule before someone skips

Clients cancel. Decide the rule while nobody is upset, and use it the same way every time. An example rule you can replace:

  • If they tell you the day before, you offer the next open slot on their usual weekday, or the next day you are already in that area.
  • If they tell you the morning of, the slot is released and the next visit is their following regular day, unless you happen to have a hole later in the week.
  • If they pause for a trip, mark the card paused and write the date they asked to restart. Do not leave them on the grid as if you are going.
  • If they skip twice in a row without a restart date, ask whether they want to stay on the route. Then update the card to active or ended.

Put the rule in the message you send when someone cancels, in one or two sentences. A rule that only lives in your head becomes a different rule for the client who argues.

Confirm the day before

Send one text the day before a recurring visit. It can be the same every time:

"This is [Company]. We are planned for [address] tomorrow between [window]. Reply if the code, the pets, or the window has changed. If you need to skip, tell us today so we can follow our reschedule rule."

You do not need a clever sentence. You need the window and a way for them to stop you from arriving to a house that is empty of the key you expected. Pair this with how to reduce no-shows if you also do one-time appointments. A recurring route fails more often from a changed code than from a stranger who forgot.

If you have an employee on the route, they get the same card facts the night before: address, code, pets, duration, and anything the client changed. They should not have to ask you from the driveway.

Close the visit the same day

When the clean is done, mark the grid and send the invoice or the "done" note you already use. A recurring business goes sideways when the work happened and the billing waited until you remembered. The note can be short: "Finished at [address] today. The next planned visit is [date]." If something in the home needs a repair that is not your job, say what you saw in one sentence and leave the repair to them. Do not turn a cleaning route into a handyman promise you did not sell.

Once a week, read the grid for the following week. Check paused cards that should restart, new clients who still have no duration, and any day that has more hours than you can work. Suppose Friday shows three homes and the durations on the cards add up past the hours you are willing to be out. That arithmetic is yours to do with your own hours. Move a home before Friday morning, using the skip rule's cousin: you offer a different window, you do not simply arrive late and apologize.

Add a new client without breaking the week

A new inquiry is not on the route until the card exists and you can see the drive from the previous stop. Ask for address, home size in their words, pets, access, and the weekdays they can allow. Then look at the grid. Offer one real window. If you do not have one, say so and offer the first week you do. A waiting list is a column on the same sheet, with the date they asked, not a stack of unread messages.

Price stays what you agreed on the card. This guide does not suggest rates. If the first clean shows the home takes longer than you allowed, talk about the price or the scope before the second visit, and write down what you both decided.

Questions about recurring cleaning schedules

What if every client wants Friday?

The grid is the answer you can show yourself. Offer Friday only while Friday has a real gap next to the other durations. Everyone else gets the nearest day you are already working in their area, or a later Friday. Say that plainly when they inquire.

Should I charge for a late cancellation?

Only if you already decided to, and you said so before the cancellation. Put it in the skip rule and in the day-before text. Do not invent a fee in the moment. If you do not charge, the rule is the reschedule you already wrote.

Can I run this from a paper planner?

Yes. One planner, client cards on paper or in a note, and a weekly look at the next seven days. If a second cleaner cannot see the planner, take a photo of the relevant day or move that week to a shared note. Two planners means two routes.

How do I handle a client who pauses every holiday?

Mark paused, write the restart date they gave you, and take them off the working grid until that date. The week before the restart, confirm as if they were a returning visit, because codes and pets change while people are away.
